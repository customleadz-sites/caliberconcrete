Sidewalks & Walkways

Concrete sidewalks & walkways in Northwest Arkansas

The front walk is the first ten steps a guest takes on your property. Caliber pours walkways that are straight, level, pitched to drain and safe to walk on wet — plus steps, side paths and the small slabs that make a yard work.

Walkways, paths, steps and small slabs

Sidewalk work covers more than the walk from the driveway to the front door. Caliber pours:

  • Front entry walks — straight, curved or offset, sized to the house.
  • Side-yard and backyard paths — from the driveway to the gate, around the house, out to a shed or shop.
  • Steps and landings — poured with the walk so there are no loose pieces to shift.
  • Stoops and porch landings at exterior doors.
  • Small slabs — HVAC pads, trash-can pads, a spot for the grill, a base for a small shed.

These are smaller jobs than a driveway, and plenty of contractors don't want them. Caliber does. They're often the projects that make the biggest day-to-day difference, and they're a good way to find out how we work before you hand us something bigger.

What makes a walkway last

Base and thickness

Even a walkway gets a compacted gravel base. Walks are typically poured four inches thick with reinforcement so they stay flat instead of tilting into trip edges as the ground moves. If a section will see a wheelbarrow, a riding mower or a vehicle tire, we treat it like a driveway.

Pitch and drainage

A walk that holds water is a walk that ices over in January. Every walkway is pitched slightly to one side so water sheds off, and we route it away from the foundation and any doorway.

Joints

Control joints on a walkway are spaced close together — roughly every four to five feet on a four-foot walk — so the inevitable shrinkage cracks land in the joints and the walk stays looking crisp. The joint pattern is laid out to look deliberate, often lined up with steps or a landing.

Finish

Broom finish is the standard: it gives traction when wet and reads clean. A smooth-troweled border along the edges is a nice touch that frames the walk. For a front entry that should make an impression, a stamped or colored walk ties into a stamped porch or patio.

Steps done right

Poured concrete steps are one of the sturdiest things you can put in a yard, but only if the proportions are right. Logan sizes risers and treads so they're comfortable to climb and consistent from step to step — the inconsistency is what trips people. Steps are formed and poured as part of the walk, and they can be broom-finished or stamped to match. Where a walk meets a porch or patio we pour a proper landing so the door swings clear.

Tying into existing concrete

Adding a section to an older walk or meeting an existing porch slab takes a little care. We match the finished height so there's no lip, pitch the new section the same direction, and finish it to blend as closely as the old concrete allows. New concrete will look brighter than weathered concrete for a season or two; that evens out.

Timeline

Most walkway jobs are a one-to-two-day project: form and base one day, pour the next, with a quick return to cut joints and pull forms. Small slabs can sometimes be worked in alongside another job. Logan can usually start within about a week of the estimate, weather allowing.

Questions about sidewalks & walkways

How wide should a front walk be?

Three feet is the minimum that feels comfortable for one person; four feet lets two people walk side by side and looks more in proportion with most homes. Logan will stake it out so you can see it before the pour.

Can you pour steps as part of a walkway?

Yes. Steps are formed and poured with the walk so they're one piece, with risers and treads sized to be comfortable and safe.

Do you match an existing sidewalk?

If you're adding a section to an existing walk we match the height, width and finish as closely as the old concrete allows and tie the new section in cleanly.

Will a new walkway be slippery when wet?

Not with a broom finish — the texture gives shoes traction. It's the standard finish for walkways for exactly that reason.

How long before I can walk on it?

About 24 hours for foot traffic, though we'll ask you to keep off the edges a bit longer. Full strength takes a few weeks.
