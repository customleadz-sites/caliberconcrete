What we do

Walkway work we handle

The front walk is the first ten steps a guest takes on your property. These are smaller jobs than a driveway, and plenty of contractors don't want them. Caliber does. They're often the projects that make the biggest day-to-day difference.

Front entry walks

Straight, curved or offset, sized to the house. Three feet is the minimum that feels comfortable; four feet lets two people walk side by side and looks in proportion with most homes.

Side-yard & backyard paths

From the driveway to the gate, around the house, out to a shed or shop. Anywhere you're tired of walking through mud.

Steps, stoops & landings

Poured with the walk so there are no loose pieces to shift. Risers and treads sized to be comfortable and consistent, with a proper landing where the walk meets a door.

Small slabs & pads

HVAC pads, trash-can pads, a spot for the grill, a base for a small shed. Quick jobs that can often be worked in alongside another pour.

Construction process

How we build a walkway

A walk that holds water is a walk that ices over in January, and a walk on a soft base tilts into trip edges. Here's the order we work in and why each step matters to you.

01

Stake it out

Logan comes out, walks the route with you and stakes it. You see the width, the curve and where it meets the steps or the drive before anything is dug. We talk about what the walk will carry: foot traffic only, or a wheelbarrow, a riding mower, a vehicle tire. If a section takes wheels, we build it like a driveway. You get a written estimate from that visit, free.

02

Base, forms and reinforcement

Sod and topsoil come out and a gravel base goes in and gets compacted, even on a walkway. Forms are set for straight lines or clean curves, at a height that meets the porch, the steps and the driveway with no lip. Every walk is pitched slightly to one side so water sheds off and away from the foundation and any doorway. Then the steel goes in. Walks are typically poured four inches thick with reinforcement so they stay flat instead of tilting as the ground moves. Steps and landings are formed with the walk so they're poured as one piece.

03

Pour and finish

The concrete is placed, screeded level with the forms and floated. Then it's finished. Broom finish is the standard: it gives traction when wet and reads clean. A smooth-troweled border along the edges frames the walk. For a front entry that should make an impression, a stamped or colored walk ties into a stamped porch or patio. Logan is on the slab for this part.

04

Joints and cleanup

Control joints on a walkway are spaced close together, roughly every four to five feet on a four-foot walk, so the shrinkage cracks land in the joints and the walk stays crisp. The pattern is laid out to look deliberate, often lined up with steps or a landing. Forms come out, edges get backfilled, and the yard is put back.

Finish options

How the surface can look

Walkways are mostly about traction and a clean line. These three cover nearly every job.

Broom finish

The standard for walks. A light, even texture for traction when it's wet or icy, hides minor marks and cleans up with a hose.

Troweled border

A smooth-troweled band along each edge with a broom-finished field. A small touch that frames the walk and makes the edges read sharp.

Stamped or colored

For a front entry that should match a stamped porch or patio, or integral color so the walk matches the brick or trim. See stamped & decorative.

Timeline & expectations

What to expect from first call to first walk

Scheduling

Logan can usually start within about a week of the estimate, weather allowing. Small slabs can sometimes be worked in alongside another job. No pours in the rain or below freezing, and you'll always hear about a delay before, not after.

On the job

Most walkway jobs are a one-to-two-day project: form and base one day, pour the next, with a quick return to cut joints and pull forms. We'll keep a path to your door open while the new one cures.

After the pour

Foot traffic after about 24 hours, though we'll ask you to keep off the edges a bit longer. Full strength takes a few weeks. New concrete looks brighter than old for a season or two; that evens out.

How wide should a front walk be?

Three feet is the minimum that feels comfortable for one person; four feet lets two people walk side by side and looks more in proportion with most homes. Logan will stake it out so you can see it before the pour.

Can you pour steps as part of a walkway?

Yes. Steps are formed and poured with the walk so they're one piece, with risers and treads sized to be comfortable and safe.

Do you match an existing sidewalk?

If you're adding a section to an existing walk we match the height, width and finish as closely as the old concrete allows and tie the new section in cleanly, with no lip.

Will a new walkway be slippery when wet?

Not with a broom finish. The texture gives shoes traction. It's the standard finish for walkways for exactly that reason.

Do you pour small slabs like an HVAC pad or shed base?

Yes. Small pads are quick jobs and can often be worked in alongside another pour. Tell Logan what's going on it so it gets the right thickness.

How long before I can walk on it?

About 24 hours for foot traffic, though we'll ask you to keep off the edges a bit longer. Full strength takes a few weeks.
