Stamped & Decorative

Stamped & decorative concrete in Northwest Arkansas

The look of cut stone, slate or cobble — in one solid slab that won't shift, won't grow weeds and won't need re-leveling. Stamped and colored concrete is how a patio, front walk or driveway border becomes the thing people notice.

What stamped concrete is

Stamped concrete is a regular concrete slab — same base, same reinforcement, same thickness — with pattern and texture pressed into the surface while it's still workable, and color added so it reads like natural stone or pavers. The result is a single, solid, decorative surface. You get the look of a flagstone patio without the joints that heave and the weeds that come up between them.

Caliber uses it most on patios, porch slabs, steps and front entry walks, and as borders and bands on driveways where a full stamped drive isn't in the plan but you want the entrance to look finished.

How a stamped job comes together

Choosing the pattern and color

This is the fun part. Logan brings sample photos and can get sample boards so you can see pattern and color together, in daylight, against your house. The patterns people ask for most:

  • Flagstone — large, irregular stones. Relaxed and natural; the pattern on the patio in the photos on this page.
  • Ashlar slate — rectangular cut-stone look. Cleaner and more formal.
  • Cobblestone — classic, works well on borders and driveway aprons.
  • Wood plank — the look of boards in a slab that won't rot.
  • Seamless texture — stone texture with no pattern lines, for a subtle finish.

Color

There are two ways to color concrete, and most stamped jobs use both. Integral color is mixed into the whole batch, so the color goes all the way through and can't wear off. A release or accent color is applied at the surface during stamping and settles into the texture, giving the stone a natural, two-tone depth. Logan will steer you toward combinations that look like real stone rather than painted concrete.

Prep and pour

Everything that matters on a plain slab matters more on a stamped one, because the surface is meant to be looked at. Compacted base, reinforcement, correct pitch for drainage, and clean forms. The pour is timed carefully — stamping has to happen in a window while the concrete is firm enough to hold the pattern but soft enough to take it.

Stamping

Texture mats are laid and pressed in sequence across the slab. Edges, steps and the spots the mats can't reach get hand-detailed so the pattern carries through. Joints are planned into the layout so they follow pattern lines where possible instead of cutting across them.

Sealing

After curing, the slab is cleaned and sealed. The sealer brings out the color and protects the surface. On patios, steps and anywhere wet, a non-slip additive goes in the sealer so the finished surface is safe underfoot.

Decorative options without full stamping

Not every job needs the full treatment. Some of the best-looking work is a plain broom-finished field with a decorative edge:

  • A stamped or colored border around a patio or along a driveway.
  • A band across a driveway apron or at the top of a walk.
  • Integral color on a broom-finished slab so it matches the brick or trim.
  • A smooth troweled edge framing a broom-finished walk.

These give you most of the look for a fraction of the labor, and they're easy to combine with a stamped patio so everything ties together.

Keeping it looking new

Sweep it, hose it off, and reseal every two to three years — more often for a driveway that sees tires daily. That's the whole maintenance list. No weeding joints, no re-leveling pavers, no staining boards.

Questions about stamped & decorative

What patterns can you stamp?

The popular ones are flagstone, ashlar slate, cobblestone, wood plank and seamless textures. Logan can show you sample boards and photos so you can pick a pattern that fits the house.

Is stamped concrete slippery?

A sealed stamped surface can be slicker than broom finish when wet. We add a non-slip additive to the sealer on patios, steps and pool-adjacent areas so it's safe underfoot.

Does stamped concrete need maintenance?

Keep it swept and reseal it every two to three years to keep the color rich and protect the surface. That's about it — no weeds, no shifting pavers, no staining a deck.

Can you color concrete without stamping it?

Yes. Integral color — color mixed through the whole batch — works on a plain broom-finished slab too. It's a simple way to make a driveway or walk match the house.

Do you do exposed aggregate?

No. Caliber does broom, stamped and colored finishes and keeps the focus on doing those three well.
