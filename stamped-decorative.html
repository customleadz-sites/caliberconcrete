What we do

Decorative work we handle

Stamped concrete is a regular slab, same base, same steel, same thickness, with pattern and texture pressed into the surface while it's still workable and color added so it reads like natural stone. You get the look of a flagstone patio without the joints that heave.

Stamped patios & porch slabs

The most common stamped job. A patio or covered porch floor in flagstone, slate or cobble, colored to match the house. See patios.

Stamped steps & fire pit patios

Steps carry the texture around a corner, and a fire pit patio with a curved edge is one of the best-looking stamped jobs there is. Formed and poured as one piece.

Borders & bands

A stamped or colored border around a patio, a band across a driveway apron, or an edge along a walk. Most of the look for a fraction of the labor.

Integral color

Color mixed through the whole batch on a plain broom-finished slab, so a driveway or walk matches the brick or trim and the color never wears off.

Construction process

How a stamped job comes together

Everything that matters on a plain slab matters more on a stamped one, because the surface is meant to be looked at. Here's the order we work in and why each step matters to you.

01

Pick the pattern and color

This is the fun part. Logan brings sample photos and can get sample boards so you see pattern and color together, in daylight, against your house. Most stamped jobs use two colors: integral color mixed through the whole batch, and a release or accent color worked into the texture at the surface for a natural two-tone depth. Logan steers you toward combinations that look like real stone rather than painted concrete.

02

Prep and pour

Compacted gravel base, steel reinforcement, correct pitch for drainage and clean forms. The pour is timed carefully, because stamping has to happen in a window while the concrete is firm enough to hold the pattern but soft enough to take it.

03

Stamping

Texture mats are laid and pressed in sequence across the slab. Edges, steps and the spots the mats can't reach get hand-detailed so the pattern carries through. Logan is on the slab for every mat.

04

Joints and detailing

Control joints are planned into the layout so they follow pattern lines where possible instead of cutting across them. Steps and borders are finished by hand so the pattern reads as one piece.

05

Sealing and cleanup

After curing, the slab is cleaned and sealed. The sealer brings out the color and protects the surface. On patios, steps and anywhere wet, a non-slip additive goes in the sealer so it's safe underfoot. Forms come out, edges are backfilled, and the yard is put back.

Patterns & options

Patterns people ask for most

Every pattern can be paired with any color combination, and any of them works as a border on a plain slab.

Ashlar slate

Rectangular cut-stone look. Cleaner and more formal. Suits a front entry, a porch floor or a patio around a fire pit.

Cobblestone

Classic and a little busier. Works on a full patio, on borders, driveway aprons and the band at the top of a walk.

Wood plank

The look of boards in a slab that won't rot, stain or splinter. A good fit for a dining patio or under a covered porch.

Flagstone

Large, irregular stones. Relaxed and natural, and a pattern that carries well around steps.

Seamless texture

Stone texture with no pattern lines, for a subtle finish that still has grip and depth.

Borders and bands

A stamped or colored edge on a broom-finished field. Easy to combine with a stamped patio so everything ties together.

Timeline & expectations

What to expect from first call to first evening on it

Scheduling

Logan can usually start within about a week of the estimate, weather allowing. Stamped work is more weather-sensitive than a plain pour, so a wet or very hot week can move the date. You'll always hear about it before, not after.

On the job

Prep and forms one day, the pour and stamping the next, then a return to cut joints, clean and seal once the slab has cured. Count on a day more than the same job in plain broom finish.

After the pour

Walk on it after a day or two. Furniture once the sealer has cured, and we'll give you exact timing. Then: sweep it, hose it off, and reseal every two to three years. That's the whole maintenance list.

What patterns can you stamp?

The popular ones are flagstone, ashlar slate, cobblestone, wood plank and seamless textures. Logan can show you sample boards and photos so you can pick a pattern that fits the house.

Is stamped concrete slippery?

A sealed stamped surface can be slicker than broom finish when wet. We add a non-slip additive to the sealer on patios, steps and pool-adjacent areas so it's safe underfoot.

Does stamped concrete need maintenance?

Keep it swept and reseal it every two to three years to keep the color rich and protect the surface. That's about it. No weeds, no shifting pavers, no staining a deck.

Can you color concrete without stamping it?

Yes. Integral color, mixed through the whole batch, works on a plain broom-finished slab too. It's a simple way to make a driveway or walk match the house.

Can you stamp just a border on my driveway or patio?

Yes, and it's a popular choice. A broom-finished field with a stamped or colored border gives you most of the look for a fraction of the labor.

Do you do exposed aggregate?

No. Caliber does broom, stamped and colored finishes and keeps the focus on doing those three well.
