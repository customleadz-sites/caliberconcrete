What we do

Patio work we handle

A patio is where the backyard actually gets used. Pavers shift and grow weeds in the joints, decks need staining and eventually rot. A properly built concrete patio is one solid surface that sits level for decades and needs almost nothing from you.

Backyard patios

Broom-finished and simple, or stamped and colored to be the centerpiece. Sized for how you'll actually use it: a table, a grill, a seating area, a fire pit.

Covered patios & porch slabs

Slabs under a covered porch, pavilion or pergola. We coordinate with your builder so the slab and the post footings are planned together and the floor height works with the structure.

Steps & grade changes

Arkansas yards are rarely flat. Where the patio meets a slope we form and pour wide, shallow steps right into the slab, in the same finish as the patio.

Extensions & add-ons

Make an existing patio bigger, add a grill or hot tub pad, or run a walkway from the patio to the drive. Matched to the height and pitch of what's already there.

Construction process

How we build a patio

A patio that pools water against the house, or sits an inch too high at the door, is a problem for years. Here's the order we work in and why each step matters to you.

01

Lay it out with you

The first thing Logan does at the estimate is lay the patio out with you. Stakes and string on the ground show exactly how far it comes off the house and how the furniture fits. It's a lot easier to move a string than a slab. We'll talk about steps if there's a grade change, how the patio meets the door threshold, and where the hose bib, downspouts and dryer vent are so nothing ends up in the way. You get a written estimate from that visit, free.

02

Forms, steel and pour

Forms are set for straight edges and any curves, steel reinforcement goes in on chairs so it sits in the middle of the slab, and the concrete is placed and screeded level. Patios are typically poured four inches thick. If you're planning a hot tub or a heavy outdoor kitchen, say so at the estimate. That footprint gets extra thickness and steel.

03

Finish or stamp

A broom finish gives a clean, slip-resistant surface. A stamped patio gets its pattern pressed in during a short window while the concrete is firm enough to hold it, with color added so it reads like stone. Logan is on the slab for this part, not watching from the truck.

04

Joints, sealing and cleanup

Control joints are cut in a layout that looks planned, not random. Stamped patios get sealed once they've cured, with a non-slip additive in the sealer. Forms come out, the lawn edge is backfilled, and the yard is put back. The only sign we were there should be the patio.

Finish options

How the surface can look

Most patios land on one of these three, or a mix: a broom-finished field with a stamped or colored border gives you most of the look for less of the labor.

Broom finish

The clean, classic look. A light texture for traction when it's wet, easy to keep up with a hose. The most common choice for a patio that's about the furniture and the view, not the floor.

Stamped

Patterns like flagstone, ashlar slate or cobble pressed into the surface, with color through the slab and at the surface so it reads like real stone. See stamped & decorative for patterns.

Color, borders and bands

Integral color mixed through the whole batch so it never wears off. Warm tans and grays pair with brick and stone homes; charcoal looks sharp against light siding and timber. A colored or stamped border frames a plain field.

Timeline & expectations

What to expect from first call to first cookout

Scheduling

Logan can usually start within about a week of the estimate, weather allowing. Concrete doesn't get poured in the rain or below freezing, so a wet week can move the date. You'll always hear about it before, not after.

On the job

A typical patio is a two-to-three-day job: a day for excavation, base and forms, a day for the pour, and a visit back to cut joints and pull forms. Stamped work adds a day for color, stamping and sealing.

After the pour

Walk on it after a day or two, furniture after about a week. For a stamped patio the sealer needs to cure too, and we'll give you exact timing for your slab. The yard is cleaned up when we leave.

Broom finish or stamped, which should I pick?

Broom finish is clean, slip-resistant and the simplest to look after. Stamped concrete is for when the patio is meant to be seen. It mimics stone, slate or pavers in a solid slab. Many people do a broom-finish field with a stamped or colored border. Logan will walk you through both on the estimate.

Can a patio go under a covered porch or pavilion?

Yes, and it's one of our favorite jobs. We coordinate with your builder so post footings and the slab work together, and we pitch the slab so water still drains out from under the roof.

How big should a patio be?

Big enough for how you'll actually use it. A table for six with room to pull chairs out needs roughly 12 by 14 feet; a grill station and a seating area need more. Logan will lay it out with you on site with stakes and string so you can see it before anything is poured.

Will the patio drain away from my house?

It has to. Every patio we pour is pitched away from the foundation so rain and hose water run off into the yard, not against the slab or the house.

Can you pour steps as part of the patio?

Yes. Where the patio meets a slope we form and pour steps into the slab, with risers and treads sized to be comfortable and consistent. Stamped patterns carry around the steps too.

How long until I can put furniture on it?

Walk on it after a day or two, furniture after about a week. For a stamped patio the sealer needs to cure too. We'll give you exact timing for your slab.
